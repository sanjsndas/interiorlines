    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between interior design and interior architecture? +
    Interior design focuses on the selection and arrangement of finishes, furniture, lighting, and decor within an existing spatial envelope. Interior architecture involves modifying the structural or spatial elements of a building's interior — moving walls, altering ceiling heights, or reconfiguring circulation. In practice, the two disciplines overlap significantly, and many professionals work across both.
    How do I choose the right lighting for a living room? +
    Start with ambient light — a mix of ceiling fixtures and floor lamps that provide general illumination. Add task lighting near seating areas for reading. Finally, layer in accent lighting to highlight art, shelving, or architectural features. Dimmers on all circuits are essential; they allow you to adjust the mood throughout the day. Aim for warm color temperatures (2700K–3000K) in living spaces.
    What are the most common space planning mistakes? +
    The most frequent mistakes include: placing furniture against every wall (which creates a bowling-alley feeling), blocking natural circulation paths, ignoring the relationship between seating and focal points, and underestimating the space needed for doors and drawers to open. Successful space planning begins with understanding how you move through and use a room, not with furniture placement.
    Is natural stone worth it for a kitchen countertop? +
    It depends on your priorities. Marble offers unmatched character but stains and etches easily. Quartzite is harder and more resistant but can still be sensitive to acids. Granite is durable and low-maintenance but varies wildly in appearance. Soapstone is non-porous and develops a patina but requires periodic oiling. If you value character and are willing to maintain it, natural stone is unmatched. If you want zero maintenance, engineered quartz is the more practical choice.
    How can I make a small apartment feel larger? +
    Focus on four things: light, sightlines, storage, and scale. Maximize natural light and use mirrors strategically. Keep sightlines to windows unobstructed. Build in storage wherever possible to reduce visual clutter. Choose furniture with exposed legs and modest proportions rather than oversized pieces. Consistency in flooring and a restrained color palette also help spaces read as more expansive.

    About Interior Lines Journal

    Interior Lines Journal is an independent editorial publication devoted to residential architecture and interior design. We publish long-form articles, practical guides, and critical perspectives on the built environment — with a focus on how design decisions affect the way people actually live.

    We are not a design firm, a product marketplace, or a portfolio. We are a publication. Our editorial independence allows us to write honestly about what works, what doesn't, and why.

    Editorial Approach

    Every article is written with the reader in mind — whether you are a design professional seeking a deeper perspective, a homeowner planning a renovation, or simply someone who cares about the quality of everyday spaces. We prioritize clarity over jargon, evidence over trends, and usefulness over aspiration alone.
